## How this list is built

Most job-board repos are not scrapers — they are renderers. The scraping happens
in a private backend, and a GitHub Action rewrites the markdown on a cron. This
repo does the scraping itself, in the open, in [`scripts/`](/scripts).

```
sources.py    14 community trackers (SimplifyJobs, vanshb03, speedyapply,
              aprameyak, coconight01, WonOfAKind, zapplyjobs, and more)
instagram.py  job links shared in Instagram stories (zero2sudo), every 4h
ats_live.py   ~153 company ATS boards, scraped directly
   ↓
classify.py   keep only genuine ML/DS roles at the new-grad level
   ↓
aggregate.py  dedupe across sources → data/listings.json (tracks first_seen)
   ↓
verify.py     ask the employer's own ATS: still open? really entry level?
   ↓
render.py     rewrite the tables in this file, verified roles only
```

Roughly 80,000 raw postings come in per full run; about 1,300 survive the
classifier, and about 1,000 of those survive verification against the employer.

Half the trackers read are general new-grad or software-engineering lists rather
than AI ones. That is deliberate: upstream, a *Machine Learning Engineer* role is
routinely filed under "Software Engineering", and an AI-only list never surfaces
it. The classifier, not the source, decides what qualifies.

### Two scrape tiers

Boards cost wildly different amounts to read, so they run on different clocks.

| Tier | Boards | Cost | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **fast** | ~147 Greenhouse / Ashby / Lever JSON APIs — the AI labs and startups | ~7s for all of them | **hourly** |
| **heavy** | Apple, Amazon, Google, Tesla, TikTok, Uber custom career sites | ~60s | 4× daily |

The fast tier is where being early actually matters: lab and startup roles open
and close in days. Since public repos get unlimited GitHub Actions minutes, an
hourly scrape costs nothing.

### Why the classifier is the hard part

A title containing "AI" is often not an ML job — *AI Business Development
Analyst* is sales, *Thermal Engineer - AI Satellites* is a thermal job that
happens to work on a product named AI Satellites, and *System AI Engineer -
Display - ASICS Engineering* is chip design. `classify.py` filters ~99% of raw
postings out.

**The gate is a whitelist, not a blacklist.** A posting is kept only if it lands
in one of four families:

| Family | What qualifies |
|---|---|
| `ml` | ML/AI engineering and research — MLE, applied scientist, research scientist, CV, NLP, LLM, RL, recsys, ranking, perception, speech, MLOps, ML infra |
| `ds` | Data scientist, decision scientist (and quantitative researcher, which has its own section) |
| `de_ml` | Data engineering **only** with an ML specialization in the title. A plain *Data Engineer* is not on this list |
| `swe_ml` | Software engineering **only** with an ML specialization in the title, or a job description unmistakably about ML work |

Blacklists were tried first and lose to the long tail. A whitelist fails closed:
a title nobody anticipated is dropped rather than admitted.

**Software engineering titles are the hardest case,** because "AI" attaches to
jobs that involve no modelling at all: *AI-Augmented Software Engineer* writes
code with an AI assistant, *AI Security Software Engineer* secures an AI product,
*AI Prompt Engineer* and *AI Solutions Engineer* are neither. So when a title's
core role is software engineering — software engineer, backend, frontend,
platform, SRE, DevOps — it is kept only if it also names a real ML
specialization. *Software Engineer, AI/Machine Learning* stays; *Software
Engineer, AI Agent* does not. Where a source ships the job text, two independent
ML signals in the description can promote such a title instead; one passing
mention of "machine learning" in a company blurb cannot.

**Three exclusion sets run before the families are consulted,** because they
describe jobs that share ML vocabulary without being ML jobs:

- **Hardware and silicon** — ASIC, SoC, VLSI, RTL, FPGA, EDA, PCB, wafer,
  circuit, physical design, power electronics, firmware. Chip work carries "AI"
  in the title constantly, because the silicon is *for* AI.
- **Wet lab and physical science** — *Stem Cell Research Scientist* and
  *Research Scientist - Ceramist* share the research title and nothing else.
  A computational qualifier overrides this, so *Computational Genomics Research
  Scientist* survives.
- **Business, ops, and data labeling** — sales, consulting, forward-deployed,
  prompt engineering, and *Robotics Data Collection Operator*, which is a person
  moving a robot arm.

Two more rules earn their place: **robotics** counts only alongside a learning or
perception component, since *Subsea Robotics Engineer* is mechanical work; and
**bare "AI"** counts only next to an engineer, scientist, or researcher noun,
which is what separates *AI Scientist* from *AI Resident* and *AI & Data Analyst*.

**Level is a separate gate from family.** Early career means new grad through
`MAX_YEARS` (3). A stated experience requirement above that drops the role even
when the title reads junior, and that applies to every source rather than only
the raw ATS feeds. Internships, apprenticeships, German dual-study programs, and
"summer analyst" titles are excluded outright.

Every one of those rules is pinned by a case in
[`tests/test_classify.py`](/tests/test_classify.py), drawn from postings that
were actually misfiled at some point. CI runs it before each refresh.

The community trackers are curated new-grad lists, so anything on them is
already the right level. Raw ATS boards are not — they list every role at a
company. Those go through a stricter gate that demands positive evidence a role
is entry level: a stated years-of-experience figure, or explicit new-grad
language. Greenhouse and Ashby return the job description inside the listing
payload, so that evidence is free to extract; the custom career sites would need
one request per job, so they are gated on their title instead. Ambiguous
postings are dropped rather than guessed at — a stale or senior role in this
list costs you more than a missing one.

### Nothing upstream is taken at face value

A tracker's README is a snapshot of someone else's scrape. Measured across the
sources this repo reads, a quarter of the links pointed at a redirector or an
aggregator rather than the employer; roles stayed listed after closing; and
"new grad" rows turned out to require five years. So after aggregation, every
posting is checked against the employer's own applicant tracking system, and
only what that check vouches for is rendered.

**Links are resolved to the employer.** zapplyjobs' redirector currently sends
every visitor to its generic job index, even for open jobs, so its slugs are
decoded into the Ashby, Lever, Greenhouse, SmartRecruiters or Workday URL they
encode. dreamworkhq pages are read for the employer link they embed. Links to
aggregators that hide the employer behind JavaScript (jobright.ai, LinkedIn,
Indeed) cannot be verified and are not shown.

**Liveness comes from the ATS API, not an HTTP status.** Workday and Ashby
return 200 for a closed job, so a status code proves nothing.

| Where the job lives | How it is checked | Counts as closed |
|---|---|---|
| Greenhouse | `boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{board}/jobs/{id}`; `gh_jid` links via the embed endpoint | 404 |
| Lever | `api.lever.co/v0/postings/{site}/{id}` | 404, **seen twice** (confidential postings 404 while live) |
| Ashby | the org's job board, once per org per run | not listed on a board that loaded |
| Workday | `/wday/cxs/{tenant}/{site}/job/...`, or the board search when a tenant blocks job reads | 404/410, `canApply: false`, end date passed |
| SmartRecruiters, Workable | their posting APIs | 404, inactive, closed or archived |
| Amazon | `amazon.jobs/en/search.json` | absent from search, **seen twice** |
| Apple, Google, Tesla, TikTok, Uber | membership in the full board scrape the refresh already runs | absent, **seen three times** |
| Anything else | the page: closed-posting text, `validThrough`, redirect off the posting, title match | any of those, **seen twice** |

Three verdicts, not two: *live*, *closed*, and *unknown*. A 403, a timeout or
a bot wall is unknown, and unknown never hides a role that was already
verified. If more than half of one ATS's checks say closed in a single run, the
closures are discarded as an outage rather than believed.

**Experience is read from the description.** Where the ATS returns one, the
requirement is parsed from it: preferred and nice-to-have sections do not
count, `3-5 years` and `3+ years` are mid-level while `2+ years` and `3 years`
are not, degree alternatives ("PhD, or Master's and 4+ years") take the easiest
path, and independent requirements stack. An explicit *New Grad* title from the
employer outranks a years figure, which on those postings is PhD research time.

**Age is the employer's date.** Trackers re-date evergreen requisitions: one
"Data Scientist" listed upstream as posted this week had been open on the
employer's board for nine years. Anything past 90 days by the employer's own
posting date is hidden. A role that cannot be verified at all is shown for 14
days after it first appears, then hidden.

Re-checks are scheduled rather than exhaustive — live roles every 12 hours,
unverifiable ones every 6, new ones at once — so the hourly run checks a few
hundred postings. Every decision is stored on the posting in
`data/listings.json` as a `verification` block with its reason and evidence.

### Why `data/listings.json` is committed

It stores `first_seen` per role across runs. That is what makes the *New this
week* section real rather than a guess based on whatever the upstream lists
happen to say. Timestamps are stored at day granularity so hourly refreshes only
produce a diff when a listing actually changed.

Run it yourself:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 scripts/aggregate.py --ats fast   # or: --ats all, --ats none
python3 scripts/render.py
```
